## Who we are

The “apo koinou” (“together, in common”) theater group of the 1st ELME of Achaea (the local secondary-school teachers’ union) was founded in 2015, on the initiative of secondary-education teachers of Achaea and the relevant decision of the 1st ELME of Achaea. Today it numbers more than thirty (30) active members.

Because the group’s members work within education, its goal is chiefly pedagogical: to offer a strong motive for knowledge and learning, for students and colleagues alike. It is a cultural activity within the educational community and the city at large, aspiring to keep opening cracks in the complacency, the indifference, the isolation, and the barbarity of our times. Inevitably, the choice of each play and the way the group operates serve mainly this goal.

**The group operates on a non-profit basis**, within the framework of amateur creation. All performances are free of charge for the audience. Expenses are covered by the fund of the 1st ELME, by various activities (cultural trips to attend theater performances in Athens, an annual ball, and more), by the monthly contributions of members, and by the support of the Region of Western Greece and the Municipality of Patras.

Every year, without interruption, the group stages at least one major production drawn from the best-known and most influential works of world drama in theaters of Patras, and “travels” to other cities (Athens, Aigio, Pyrgos, Volos, Syros), while taking part every year in the Patras Amateur Theater Festival.

In parallel, it stages performances and tributes to great historical events that marked the course of people and societies, as well as performances forming part of the central events organized by KEDIP–Carnival Patras. It also takes part, with performances and street-theater events (*dromena*), in the commemorations organized by the Deputy Mayoralty of Culture and the Deputy Mayoralty of Education of the Municipality of Patras for the great historical moments of Achaea — the liberation of Patras from Ottoman rule, the liberation of Patras from the German occupiers, the Polytechnic uprising, and more — as well as in performances that highlight and denounce persistent social problems.

## How we work

### 1. A strict rehearsal schedule

Preparation begins in early October and lasts six to seven months. Rehearsals take place three times a week — two on weekdays and one on Sunday — each lasting one and a half to two hours. Members commit to being present at every rehearsal and every performance, from October 1 to June 30. Activity pauses during the summer months and over the Christmas and Easter holidays. All members are strictly committed to a single, punctual arrival and a single departure at the agreed time, without any deviation from the schedule.

### 2. A collective way of working

The group works collectively, with no leading actors and no extras: a self-managed collective with horizontal democracy, meaning equal treatment of all members and cooperation among all — without leaders or guides, but also without indifferent or passive members. All members share the group’s obligations equally. It is inconceivable for two or three members to run around doing all the work while the rest concern themselves with nothing but their role.

### 3. Rehearsal as ritual

Rehearsals are held with a sense of sacred ritual:

- With the utmost respect for the playwright’s work.
- With a sense of responsibility and solidarity toward our fellow actors.
- With full availability, creating — by setting the ego aside — an open space for welcoming the role; that is, for welcoming the other, the different.

No one interrupts the smooth running of a rehearsal. After each rehearsal there is always time for discussion among all members about the progress of the rehearsals and about problems arising from the building of the roles. If any problem arises that abolishes this ritual during preparation (lateness, a mood for letting off steam and horseplay, egotism, nervous outbursts, rude behavior, a half-learned role, etc.), the rehearsal stops immediately, so that the matter can be discussed solely within the framework of the group’s collective functioning. **Decisions are made only by unanimity.**

Members leave their outside microcosm (family, jobs, friends, other interests) outside the rehearsal room. Whether performing or watching, we are all a single group, focused on the rehearsal. Mobile phones remain switched off for the entire duration of the rehearsal.

The director of the “apo koinou” theater group of the 1st ELME of Achaea is the educator **Fotis Lazaris**.

## Main productions

1. *To Panigyri* (The Fair) — Dimitris Kechaidis · 2015–16
2. *The Cherry Orchard* — Anton Chekhov · 2016–17
3. *The Government Inspector* — Nikolai Gogol · 2017–18
4. *All My Sons* — Arthur Miller · 2018–19
5. *Incendies* — Wajdi Mouawad · 2019–20
6. *The Visit* — Friedrich Dürrenmatt · 2020–21
7. *The Seagull* — Anton Chekhov · 2021–22
8. *The Good Person of Szechwan* — Bertolt Brecht · 2022–23
9. *Kommatia mou pou na sas vro* (“Pieces of me, where can I find you”) — a music-theater tribute marking the centenary of the Asia Minor Catastrophe, in collaboration with the mixed choir and the Amabille Orchestra of Patras · 2022–23
10. *Mialo xourafi* (“A mind sharp as a razor”) — Nikos Tsiforos · a music-theater production in collaboration with a rebetiko ensemble and the dance department of the Municipality of Patras · 2022–23
11. *The Comedy of an Ideal Suicide* — an adaptation of Nikolai Erdman’s *The Suicide* · 2023–24
12. *Ligo prin, ligo meta to… telos* (“A little before, a little after the… end”) — a music-theater production based on the humor of Arkas, in collaboration with the Philharmonic Orchestra of the Music School of Patras · 2023–24
13. *Gynaikon pathi* (“Women’s passions”) — a dramatization of texts (poetry, theater) on the passions of women through the ages, in collaboration with the female vocal ensemble *Timbres* · 2023–24
14. *Patontas pano stin anemelia sou* (“Stepping on your carefreeness”) — documentary theater based on testimonies of fighters of the Polytechnic uprising · 2024–25
15. *Eseis ti tha kanate sti thesi mou?* (“What would you have done in my place?”) — a free adaptation of Max Frisch’s *Biedermann und die Brandstifter* (*The Fire Raisers*) · 2024–25
16. *Rhinoceros* — Eugène Ionesco · 2025–26
17. *“Ever open, ever wakeful, the eyes of my soul”* (*Πάντ’ ανοιχτά, πάντ’ άγρυπνα τα μάτια της ψυχής μου*) — a music-theater production based on excerpts from the landmark works of our national poet Dionysios Solomos, *The Woman of Zakynthos* and *The Free Besieged* · 2025–26

    Conception, dramaturgy, direction: Fotis Lazaris · Music composition, orchestration, orchestra direction: Tasos Karagiannis

## Tribute performances & events

1. **Celebration of the liberation of Patras** — street-theater events at the Patras Refuge (Katafygio) and on the Three Admirals’ Steps (October 2022, October 2023).
2. **Celebration of March 25** — street-theater events about Dionysis Karatzas, a freedom fighter from Patras, at Omonia Square (March 2022, March 2023).
3. A theater piece at the presentation of Eleni Sigalou’s poetry collection *Iouliou ochlisis* — Patras, Polyedro bookstore (March 2017) · Athens, “epi lexei” bookstore (May 2017).
4. A theater piece at the presentation of Eleni Sigalou’s poetry collection *Ekpnoes nerou* (“Exhalations of water”) — Patras, Lithografeio Theater (March 2023) · Athens, the alternative bookstore of Koukida publications (May 2023).
5. Tribute to the work of Bertolt Brecht — Polyedro bookstore (February 2023).
6. Tribute to the work of Nikolai Erdman — Polyedro bookstore (March 2024).
7. A music-theater piece in collaboration with the vocal ensemble *Timbres*, at the presentation of the poetry collection *Fomalo* (Mandragoras publications) by Maria Dalamitrou and Eleni Sigalou — Patras, Lithografeion Theater (March 2025).
8. **In the garden of Polyedro:** *“ever open, ever wakeful, the eyes of my soul”* — In one of the most emblematic, atmospheric, and historic cultural venues of Patras, a part of the soul of our performance unfolded at the event held on Monday, May 18, 2026. In a very warm and moving atmosphere, we opened the “workshop” of our preparation to the public. Speakers included the poets, educators, and members of the theater group Eleni Sigalou and Maria Dalamitrou, the composer of the performance’s music and director of the Music School of Patras Tasos Karagiannis, and the director of the performance Fotis Lazaris.
